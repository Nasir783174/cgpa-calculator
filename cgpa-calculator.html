About This Tool

What is a CGPA Calculator?

A CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average) calculator is an academic tool that measures your overall result across multiple courses, semesters, or academic years without doing the math by hand. Enter your letter grades and credit hours, and this calculator instantly turns them into an accurate cumulative GPA, the same figure that appears on your official transcript.

This CGPA calculator uses the 4.0 grading scale, one of the most widely used grading systems worldwide, where each letter grade carries a specific grade point value. It multiplies the grade points earned in each course by the corresponding credit hours, adds up the total quality points, and then divides that sum by the total number of credits attempted. The result is your CGPA, a single number that sums up your academic performance so far.

Unlike a semester GPA, which only reflects one term, CGPA represents your entire academic record across every completed semester. It gives a clearer, long-term picture of consistency and progress, which is why it carries so much weight when others evaluate your academic history.

Universities, employers, scholarship committees, and graduate admission boards regularly use CGPA to judge academic standing and eligibility for scholarships, job applications, higher-study admissions, and honors. A strong, correctly calculated CGPA can open real opportunities.

Whether you want to track your progress semester by semester, set a realistic target before your next results, or simply double-check the number your department gave you, this CGPA calculator gives you a fast, accurate, and reliable way to work out your cumulative grade point average.

Calculation Method

How Is CGPA Calculated?

CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average) is calculated by dividing the total weighted grade points earned across all completed courses by the total credit hours attempted. It serves as a comprehensive measure of a student's academic performance throughout an entire degree program rather than a single semester.

Unlike a semester GPA, which only evaluates performance during one academic term, CGPA combines results from multiple semesters and courses to provide a complete picture of long-term academic achievement.

CGPA Formula

The standard formula used to calculate CGPA is:

Total Weighted Grade Points Total Credit Hours

Understanding Credit Hours

Credit hours indicate the academic weight or value of a course. Universities assign credit hours based on factors such as classroom instruction, laboratory sessions, coursework intensity, and overall workload. Because credit hours determine a course's weight in the calculation, courses with more credit hours have a greater impact on the final CGPA.

Why CGPA Uses a Weighted Average

Many students assume CGPA is calculated by simply averaging course grades, but universities use a weighted average system instead. Under this system, each course contributes to the CGPA according to its credit value. For instance, earning an A in a 4-credit physics course contributes more grade points than earning an A in a 2-credit elective course. This approach ensures that the final CGPA accurately reflects the academic significance of each course within the curriculum.

Factors That Affect CGPA

Several academic factors can influence a student's CGPA over time: performance in high-credit courses, repeated or retaken courses, failed courses and academic penalties, credit transfer policies, additional elective courses, and institutional grading systems. Understanding these factors can help students make informed academic decisions.
