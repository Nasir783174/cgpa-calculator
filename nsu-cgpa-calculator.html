Official Policy

NSU Grading Policy and CGPA Scale

📖 Direct Answer: North South University grades every course on a 4.00-point scale published in its official Grading Policy. A = 4.00, A− = 3.70, B+ = 3.30, down to F = 0.00, and each grade is weighted by the course's credit hours. Your CGPA is your total grade points divided by your total credits counted, exactly what this calculator computes.

This tool uses that same official scale, so whether you study in the School of Business and Economics, School of Engineering and Physical Sciences, School of Health and Life Sciences, or School of Humanities and Social Sciences, entering your letter grades and credit hours here will match the CGPA shown on your RDS transcript.

Formula

How NSU Calculates Your CGPA

NSU's Office of the Controller of Examinations defines CGPA as your Total Grade Points divided by Total Credits Counted. Grade points for a course are its grade point value (from the table above) multiplied by its credit hours. This is exactly what this calculator does when you enter your courses.

CGPA =
Total Grade Points Total Credits Counted

Expanded, where each course contributes its grade point multiplied by its credit hours:

CGPA =
Σ (Grade Point × Credit Hours) Σ (Credit Hours)

Credits That Do Not Count Toward CGPA

Not every credit on your transcript affects your CGPA. Per NSU's published policy:

  • Waived courses display no grade and are not counted.
  • Transfer credits from another university count toward your degree's credit total but are excluded from your CGPA calculation.
  • Retaken courses count only once: only the better of the two grades is used in the CGPA, though both attempts remain on the transcript.

When using the calculator above, enter only the credits and grades that appear on your official transcript for CGPA purposes, and leave out waived or transferred courses.

Academic Standing and Probation at NSU

NSU defines good academic standing as a cumulative GPA of 2.00 or higher. If your CGPA falls below 2.00, the university places you on academic probation.

Good Standing
CGPA ≥ 2.00
You remain eligible to register normally and are not restricted from higher-level courses.
Academic Probation
CGPA below 2.00
You are given a maximum of three semesters to bring your CGPA back up to 2.00 or above.
Dismissal (Exclusion)

Students who cannot reach a 2.00 CGPA within three semesters of probation are dismissed from the university.

NSU Policy

NSU Retake Policy and How It Changes Your CGPA

According to NSU's official grading policy, you may retake any course in which you earned a grade of B or lower. F grades must be retaken, since an F keeps counting against your CGPA until you replace it with a passing grade in the same or an equivalent course.

  • When you retake a course, the new attempt is recorded on your transcript alongside the original.
  • Only the better of the two grades is used to calculate your CGPA.
  • The original, lower grade still appears on your transcript for record-keeping, but it no longer affects your CGPA once you have a better retake grade.
  • Retaking a course means registering and paying tuition and fees for it again, the same as any other course.

In this calculator, model a retake by entering only the grade you want counted for that course. If you originally got a C in a 3-credit course and retook it for a B+, enter one course row with B+ and 3 credits, since the C no longer factors into your CGPA.

Degree Rules

Graduation CGPA Requirements at NSU

To qualify for an undergraduate degree at NSU, university policy requires:

  • A minimum of 120 credits completed, with at least 60 of those credits earned at NSU as a degree-admitted student.
  • Completion of all course requirements for the degree, including general education, core, and concentration/major or minor courses.
  • An overall CGPA of at least 2.00 to qualify for the degree.
  • A CGPA of at least 2.50 within your Concentration/Major and Minor category, if you are graduating with a declared concentration, major, or minor.

Graduate degrees have their own thresholds set by each school and department; check with your program office for the exact CGPA required for your MSc, MBA, or other graduate degree, since these vary by discipline.

Recognition

Latin Honors and Gold Medals at NSU

NSU recognizes graduating undergraduates with three levels of Latin honors printed on the transcript: Summa Cum Laude, Magna Cum Laude, and Cum Laude, awarded based on final CGPA. The exact CGPA cut-off for each level is set by the Office of the Controller of Examinations and reviewed periodically, so confirm the current thresholds directly with that office before assuming you qualify.

Separately, NSU awards a Chancellor's Gold Medal to the single highest-CGPA graduate and Vice Chancellor's Gold Medals to the second-highest CGPA graduate from each school, alongside a Valedictorian recognition. These awards are decided on your final CGPA at the point of convocation, along with a clean disciplinary record and steady progression through your program without semester gaps.

If your goal is a Latin honor or a gold medal, track your CGPA every semester with the calculator above so you always know exactly how much room, or how little, you have left before your final transcript is finalized.

Terminology

GPA vs CGPA at NSU: What Is the Difference?

At NSU, GPA (sometimes shown as SGPA on other calculators) refers to your grade point average for a single semester or trimester. CGPA is your cumulative result across every semester you have completed so far. NSU's own transcript and academic policy documents mostly use the single term "GPA" to refer to your cumulative standing, which is why you will see phrases like "a GPA of less than 2.00" used for probation even though it means your overall, cumulative figure.

The calculator above keeps both numbers visible at the same time: each semester box shows that semester's own SGPA, while the sidebar shows your running overall CGPA across every semester you have entered.

Avoid These

Common Mistakes When Calculating Your NSU CGPA

Counting Both Attempts of a Retaken Course

If you retook a course, only the better grade counts toward your CGPA. Entering both the original low grade and the retake grade will understate your real CGPA. Enter the retaken course only once, using the better grade.

Including Waived or Transferred Credits

Waived courses and credits transferred from another university count toward your total credits for graduation but are excluded from your CGPA. Leave these out of the calculator entirely, or your result will not match your official RDS transcript.

Ignoring Credit Hours

NSU's CGPA is credit-weighted, not a simple average of your grade points. A 4-credit course with a B+ affects your CGPA more than a 1-credit lab with an A. Always enter the correct credit hours for each course.

Converting Percentages Instead of Using the Letter Grade

Course instructors set their own numerical cut-offs within university guidelines, so a raw percentage does not always map to the grade table above exactly. Use the letter grade that actually appears on your transcript rather than converting a percentage yourself.

Mixing Up NSU's Scale With the Public University (UGC) Scale

NSU's 4.00-point, credit-weighted system is different from the flat grading scale used by many public universities and National University (NU) affiliated colleges in Bangladesh. If you studied under that system instead, use our CGPA Calculator, which follows the common public-university grading scale, so your result matches your actual transcript.

Same Scale

Do BRAC, IUB, UIU, EWU or AIUB Use the Same Scale?

Yes. BRAC University, Independent University, Bangladesh (IUB), United International University (UIU), East West University (EWU), and American International University-Bangladesh (AIUB) all publish a similar North American-style 4.00 grading scale, with A = 4.00 down to F = 0.00 and 0.30-point plus/minus modifiers. Students at these universities can use the exact same calculator on this page.

Because individual grading policies still differ slightly, particularly around retake rules and probation thresholds, always cross-check your final CGPA against your own university's official grading policy or student portal, rather than assuming every rule on this page carries over exactly.
